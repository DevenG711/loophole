Loophole
How it works Download

You were about to pick up your phone.

Loophole is a small Mac app that learns what your work looks like in the minute before you reach for your phone. The next time it looks like that, it offers you a 5-minute break first.

Download for Mac (.zip) Read the code on GitHub

Free and open source. You run it with Python.

It learns your pattern, not a list of rules

  1. It learns from your own pickups

    A one-step iPhone Shortcut tells your Mac whenever you open Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X, Reddit or Snapchat. Each time, Loophole keeps a snapshot of the minute before.

  2. It steps in before you reach for it

    When right now looks like one of those snapshots, or you’re picking up your phone far more than usual, the island offers a 5-minute break. It stays quiet until it has 5 pickups to learn from.

  3. It catches slips straight away

    Open YouTube, Netflix, Reddit or Messages on your laptop, or a time-waster on your phone, and the island says so at once. That time counts as a break, not a slip. If the video really is for work, one click lets that video through.

When, then where

When are you about to stop?

Your Mac works this out on its own. Before each pickup it saves a snapshot: your typing and scrolling trends, how long you’d been on one window, how much you switched apps, and how stalled you looked. It counts keys and clicks, never what you type. Every 30 seconds it checks whether now looks like one of those snapshots.

It also keeps count. If you’ve picked up your phone at least 3 times in the last 10 minutes, and that’s at least twice your usual rate, that’s a warning sign too.

Where would you go next?

That one goes to BehaviorGPT, a model built to predict a person’s next action. Loophole sends it your last few apps and page titles and gets back a ranked list of likely next moves. The top time-waster on that list is the “Instagram looks like your next move” part of the nudge.

A nudge needs one of those two warning signs, and you must have used the keyboard or mouse in the last 10 seconds. After a nudge, it waits about your usual time between pickups before it asks again.

Running in about five minutes

You’ll need a Mac. No API key: Loophole comes with one. An iPhone is optional, but it’s how Loophole learns your pickups.

Get the .zip

  1. Unzip it. Open Terminal, type cd and a space, drag the loophole-main folder onto the window, and press Return.

  2. Run setup. It installs everything, makes your settings file, and prints your private phone link (it also copies it for you). If your Mac needs a newer Python, it tells you the one command to run.

    bash setup.sh

  3. Start it. A grey dot shows up in your menu bar. Keep the Terminal window open while it runs.

    bash start.sh

  4. Set up your iPhone (optional, 5 minutes). In the Shortcuts app, make one automation for all your distracting apps: Automation › + › App, tick every app you want watched, Is Opened, Run Immediately. Its one action is Get Contents of URL: paste your phone link, set Method to POST and Request Body to Form, and add a text field with key app whose value is Shortcut Input (pick it from the bar above the keyboard). That sends the name of whichever app you opened. Lost the link? Menu › Phone setup › Copy phone link. The phone setup guide has every tap and a test.

  5. Change break length, session lengths and more in the settings file: menu › Phone setup › Open settings file. The settings list explains each one.

To look back at a session, there’s a dashboard. It shows the apps you used, BehaviorGPT’s guesses, each nudge and each phone pickup on one timeline.

.venv/bin/streamlit run dashboard/app.py
