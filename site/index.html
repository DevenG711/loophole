You were about to pick up your phone.

Loophole is a small Mac app that notices when your work starts to stall. If a time-waster also looks like where you’re headed, it offers you a proper break before you get there.

Download for Mac (.zip) Read the code on GitHub

The zip is the source code. You run it with Python.

Mostly, it stays out of your way

Click the dot and pick 25, 50 or 90 minutes. Here’s everything that can happen after that.

  1. Green

    Work is moving along. Nothing happens.

  2. Amber

    You’ve slowed down, or a time-waster looks likely. Only the colour changes.

  3. Red

    Both of those at once. A nudge is a few seconds away.

  4. Nudge

    Take the break, wave it off, or admit the phone is already in your hand.

  5. Break

    A countdown sits where the dot was. You’re allowed to enjoy it.

  6. Back

    When time’s up, a dialog offers to bring your work app to the front.

Open the menu whenever you want the numbers behind the colour. You’ll see how long you’ve been focused, whether your typing is picking up or fading, which app BehaviorGPT expects next, and when you last took a break.

When, then where

When are you about to stop?

Your Mac works this out on its own, every 30 seconds. It compares your typing speed with your own last ten minutes. It also looks at idle time, scrolling with no typing, bursts followed by long pauses, app switching, and how long it’s been since your last break.

Where would you go next?

That one goes to BehaviorGPT, a model built to predict a person’s next action. Loophole sends it your last eight events and gets back a ranked list of likely next moves. All Loophole checks is how high the first time-waster sits.

A nudge needs all four of these at once: you’re stalling, a time-waster is in the top 10, you touched the keyboard or mouse in the last 10 seconds, and there hasn’t been a nudge for 20 minutes.

Titles say more than sites

A rule that only knows app names sees every YouTube tab as the same thing. BehaviorGPT reads the page title too, so a tutorial lands close to plain coding and prank videos land far from it.

Its blind spots

BehaviorGPT doesn’t notice pauses, the gaps between events, or the time of day. We tested each one. That’s why the timing side never leaves your Mac.

We haven’t measured how well it works yet.Treat it as an experiment you get to run on yourself.

Is this a keylogger?

No. macOS already keeps a running count of key presses, and that one number is all Loophole reads.

Sent out

  • App names
  • YouTube and Reddit page titles
  • Names of apps you open on your phone

These go to BehaviorGPT, and Loophole tells it not to save them as training events. Phone opens reach your laptop through ntfy.sh, on a topic name only you know.

Stays on your Mac

  • Counts of keys, clicks and scrolls
  • Idle time
  • Addresses and titles of your other browser tabs
  • Your scores and session log

Running in about five minutes

You’ll need macOS, Python 3.11 or newer, and a BehaviorGPT API key from UnboxAI. An iPhone is optional.

Get the .zip

  1. Unzip it and open Terminal in the new folder.

    cd loophole-main

  2. Install what it needs. No uv? pip install -e . works too.

    uv sync

  3. Make your settings file, then open .env and fill in UNBOXAI_API_KEY and NTFY_TOPIC.

    cp .env.example .env

  4. Start it. The dot shows up in your menu bar.

    .venv/bin/python -m loophole.menubar

  5. Optional: add one Shortcuts automation on your iPhone that pings your laptop when Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X, Reddit or Snapchat opens. The phone setup guide walks you through it.

To look back at a finished session, there’s a Streamlit dashboard. It lays out the apps you used, your slowdown score, BehaviorGPT’s guesses, each nudge and each pickup.

.venv/bin/streamlit run dashboard/app.py
